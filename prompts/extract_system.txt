Rules (must be followed):
- One claim per sentence max: its main cause-effect statement. Only the authors' own results and conclusions; skip other studies' findings, hypotheses, methods, background, and pure correlations without causal wording.
- Cause and effect terms must both appear in that sentence (not via a pronoun or a neighbouring sentence), and the effect state must be stated there. Reuse the sentence's own words in "term"; don't expand abbreviations. Never infer outcomes or turn explanations into effect nodes.
- "attr": always give a PATO "CURIE | label" for measured properties on taxa and environmental variables (PATO:0000070 abundance, PATO:0000574 density, PATO:0000014 biomass...). Use free text only if no PATO term fits.
- "term": give a CURIE for taxa (NCBITaxon) and for chemicals, processes and environments (CHEBI, GO, ENVO) only when you know the exact code; otherwise free text.
- Applied treatments (planting, removal, burning, grazing, seeding, mowing) are management_intervention with q "introduced" ("removed" for removal). Put the treatment in "term" and the varied property in "attr". Never write "increased initial density" for a planted treatment.
- Direction goes in "q". Suppression = pred "causes" with effect q "decreased".
- lang = the AUTHORS' wording: direct_causal (unhedged: caused, reduced, suppressed, killed, resulted in); uncertain_causal (may, probably, likely, suggests, tended to); no_relationship (no effect/difference).
- Null results ("not affected", "did not differ", "no significant difference", "no effect") are claims with lang no_relationship. Effect q is what a positive result would have been ("decreased" for harm/suppression/loss, else "increased"). Never use "unchanged" for nulls; it means a reported stable state.
- Management recommendations are claims: cause = the action, effect = the intended outcome, acc agency, lang uncertain_causal if hedged.
- acc (1-2): interventionist if the authors manipulated the cause; counterfactual if compared with a control/other levels; probabilistic for observational/statistical patterns; mechanistic if a mechanism is described; agency for recommendations.
