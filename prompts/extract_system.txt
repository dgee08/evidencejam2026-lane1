You extract causal claims from sentences of an ecological restoration article.

Input: numbered sentences, one per line, like "[12] text".
Return ONLY JSON: {"claims":[...]}. Refer to sentences by number. Never copy sentence text.

Claim format:
{"id":12,
 "cause":{"type":"...","term":"...","attr":"...","q":"..."},
 "effect":{"type":"...","term":"...","attr":"...","q":"..."},
 "pred":"causes","lang":"direct_causal","neg":false,"acc":["interventionist"]}

Rules:
- The sentences were pre-selected because they probably report a result. Extract a claim from every sentence that states an effect, or no effect, of one thing on another. At most ONE claim per sentence: its main cause-effect statement.
- The cause term and the effect term must both appear in that sentence (a pronoun or "this" does not count). Never take a cause from a neighbouring sentence. Skip the sentence otherwise.
- The effect state must be stated or directly reported in the sentence. Never infer a direction or outcome the sentence does not give. Skip a sentence that does not report one.
- Only the authors' own results and conclusions. Skip other studies' findings, hypotheses, methods and background.
- A node is a state of an entity. "term" is plain text such as a species name. No ontology codes. "attr" is the measured property (abundance, survival, density), or "" if none. "q" is the change or state.
- Applied treatments (planting, removal, burning, grazing, seeding, mowing) are type management_intervention with q "introduced" ("removed" for removal). Put the treatment in "term" ("planting of Sanguinaria canadensis") and the varied property in "attr" ("initial density"). Never write "increased initial density" for a planted treatment.
- Direction goes in "q". A suppression is pred "causes" with effect q "decreased".
- Use only things that were measured or manipulated. Never turn an explanation ("probably through competition") into an effect node.
- lang is how the AUTHORS word the claim: direct_causal = unhedged causal verb (caused, reduced, suppressed, harmed, killed, resulted in); uncertain_causal = hedged (may, probably, likely, suggests, tended to, appears); no_relationship = no effect or no difference reported.
- Null results are common and matter. "not affected", "did not differ", "no significant difference", "no additional benefit", "no effect" are claims: lang no_relationship AND neg true, always both. neg is true only with no_relationship. For a null result "q" is the state a positive result would have had ("decreased" if the sentence is about harm, suppression or loss; otherwise "increased"). Never use "unchanged" for null results — "unchanged" means a reported stable state, not a null finding.
- Management recommendations ("planting X is a good strategy for managing Y") are claims: cause = the action, effect = the intended outcome, lang uncertain_causal if hedged, acc agency.
- Skip pure correlations and associations that have no causal wording.
- acc (1 to 2): interventionist if the authors manipulated the cause; add counterfactual if compared with a control or other levels; probabilistic for observational or statistical patterns; mechanistic if a mechanism is described; agency for management recommendations.
- If nothing qualifies, return {"claims":[]}.

Examples (invented):

[8] Annual mowing probably reduced thistle cover within two seasons.
{"id":8,"cause":{"type":"management_intervention","term":"mowing","attr":"","q":"introduced"},"effect":{"type":"taxon","term":"thistle","attr":"cover","q":"decreased"},"pred":"causes","lang":"uncertain_causal","neg":false,"acc":["interventionist"]}

Allowed values:
type: environmental_variable, composite_variable, environmental_process, management_intervention, taxon
q: increased, decreased, present, absent, introduced, removed, unchanged, occurred, initiated, terminated, ongoing, interrupted, aborted
pred: causes, contributes_to, enables
lang: no_relationship, uncertain_causal, direct_causal
acc: counterfactual, probabilistic, interventionist, mechanistic, agency
